# Copy rules

## Headlines
- 8 words or fewer. One idea per poster. The renderer flags anything over 9.
- Say the outcome in the user's words: "Your notes, now a 5-minute quiz", not "AI-powered study optimisation".
- Hook types to rotate across variants: pain ("40 pages. No idea what matters."), contrarian claim ("Rereading isn't studying."), outcome ("Done in 5 minutes."), curiosity ("The 30-minute test prep plan"), number ("3 reasons it beats rereading").
- Sentence case. No hashtags or emoji in the poster itself.

## CTA
- 2 to 4 words, a verb, and exactly what happens: "Try it free", "Get it free", "Join the waitlist", "Shop the drop". Keep the same CTA across a batch unless testing it.

## Honesty (hard rules)
- No invented testimonials, ratings, user counts, school names or press logos.
- Every number needs a source the student can show. Product-internal numbers are fine if real ("our beta dashboard, Oct 2026").
- No "#1", "best", "guaranteed" or medical/financial promises.
- Comparisons name a behaviour or category, not a competitor brand, unless every row is verifiably true.
- No imitation of Instagram, Facebook or WhatsApp interfaces.
- Offers need real dates and real terms in `fine`.

## Captions and alt text (in the brief, exported to captions.csv)
- Caption: first line repeats or extends the hook (it's what shows before "more"), then one line of detail, then the CTA ("Link in bio"). 1 to 3 short lines.
- Alt text: what's on the poster in one sentence, including the headline text. Required for every post.

## Teen voice
- Write like the founder talks to a classmate: short, specific, a little funny, never cringe slang.
- Avoid corporate filler: "revolutionary", "seamless", "unlock your potential", "game-changer".
